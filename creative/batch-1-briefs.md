## #13 Could You Cover a Surprise Bill? (The Numbers)
- **Central idea:** A $400 surprise is a common problem, so a small separate bucket is worth starting.
- **Hook options:**
  - Nearly 4 in 10 wouldn't cover $400 with cash.
  - A $400 surprise splits America almost in two.
- **Try it today:** Open or name a separate "surprise" bucket.
- **Source status:** Federal Reserve SHED report on 2025, released 2026-05-13: 63% would cover a $400 expense with cash or equivalent, unchanged from 2024. Seen only through secondary coverage, so confirm in the Fed's report.
- **Not claimed:** That the other 37% can't pay at all. Some would use a credit card and pay it off, or have other access to funds. No shaming and no bank picks.
- **Comment trigger:** "What was your last surprise bill?"

## #20 Does Paying Cash Hurt More Than Card?
- **Central idea:** In one lab auction, payment method alone changed what people offered.
- **Hook options:**
  - Same item. People paying by card offered more.
  - Cash or card changed what people would pay.
- **Try it today:** Pay for one purchase with cash and notice how it feels.
- **Source status:** Prelec & Simester 2001, Marketing Letters 12(1), 5-12. Check the items, sample and size in the full text.
- **Not claimed:** That cards make everyone overspend, or anything about real-world spending. No card or bank advice. NOT FINANCIAL ADVICE on screen.
- **Comment trigger:** "Does tapping a card feel like spending?"

## #15 The 25-Hour Hold (was "24-Hour Rule")
The tested delay was 25 hours, so the episode should say 25.
- **Central idea:** A short wait didn't cool the impulse in one test; waiting about a day did lower the urge.
- **Hook options:**
  - Waiting 10 minutes didn't stop impulse buys.
  - 10 minutes failed. 25 hours cooled the urge.
- **Try it today:** Put one cart item on a 25-hour hold.
- **Source status:** Carol Moser's University of Michigan dissertation (2020). A 25-hour delay lowered felt urge and purchase intent online; a 10-minute in-lab delay on Amazon showed no significant drop. I found no journal version, and the CHI 2019 paper is not confirmed to contain this result. Say "one dissertation experiment" and get the sample size from the PDF.
- **Not claimed:** Money saved, regret avoided, or any percentage. Card limit: `urge and intent, not money saved`.
- **Comment trigger:** "What's sitting in your cart right now?"

## #24 Is Your Evening Coffee Hurting Your Sleep? (Habit Myths)
- **Central idea:** Caffeine well before bed still disturbed sleep, and people didn't notice.
- **Hook options:**
  - Coffee six hours before bed still cost sleep.
  - They lost sleep to caffeine and didn't notice.
- **Try it today:** Move your last coffee 2 hours earlier.
- **Source status:** Drake, Roehrs, Shambroom & Roth 2013, J. Clinical Sleep Medicine 9(11), 1195-1200. Free on PMC. Take the sleep-loss figure from the paper.
- **Not claimed:** A universal cutoff time ("after 5 p.m." came from press coverage). Card limit: `12 healthy sleepers, 400 mg`. No supplement advice. NOT MEDICAL ADVICE on screen.
- **Comment trigger:** "What time is your last coffee?"

## #10 Power Outage: The Numbers That Matter (was "Hacks")
The channel avoids the word "hack".
- **Central idea:** In an outage, the clock on your food starts right away, so keep the doors shut.
- **Hook options:**
  - Your fridge has 4 hours. Your freezer has 48.
  - A power cut starts a 4-hour food clock.
- **Try it today:** Find your flashlight and test the batteries.
- **Source status:** Ready.gov power outages page, read 2026-11-09 (last updated 2026-06-04). Fridge about 4 hours; **full** freezer about 48 hours; keep both closed; throw out food at 40°F or above for 2 hours or more; one flashlight per household member; generators outdoors, at least 20 feet from windows and doors.
- **Not claimed:** "Flashlights, not candles": the page doesn't mention candles, so don't use it. Card: `READY.GOV` / `GUIDANCE`, not "study". FOLLOW LOCAL GUIDANCE on screen.
- **Comment trigger:** "How long was your longest outage?"

## #8 The Phone "Brain Drain" Myth (Habit Myths)
This has to be a myth check. The popular claim is the thing being tested.
- **Central idea:** A famous finding about phones draining focus didn't reliably show up when retested.

- **Comment trigger:** "Where's your phone right now?"
