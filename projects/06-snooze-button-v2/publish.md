## 2. Description

30 minutes of snoozing cost about 6 minutes of sleep in one lab study of 31 regular snoozers, and their thinking tests right after getting up were as good or better than after an abrupt wake-up. Try it today: decide your snooze window before bed.
Source: Sundelin, T., Landry, S., & Axelsson, J. (2023/2024). Is snoozing losing? Why intermittent morning alarms are used and how they affect sleep, cognition, cortisol, and mood. Journal of Sleep Research. doi:10.1111/jsr.14054
NOT MEDICAL ADVICE. This was one lab study of 31 habitual snoozers and covers only the first moments after waking, so results may differ for you. The planning steps are a practical way to try the idea and were not tested in the study.
