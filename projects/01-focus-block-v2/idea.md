## Source (verified 2026-11-06 via PubMed + Crossref)
Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans (2001), JEP: Human Perception and Performance, 27(4), 763-797. doi:10.1037/0096-1523.27.4.763. Finding: in four lab experiments, people were slower when alternating tasks than repeating one; cost grew with rule complexity and shrank with cues. Card: "Rubinstein et al., 2001" / "SWITCHING = SLOWER" / "simple lab tasks".
Not claimed: that a text message equals exactly two measured switches (the "two" is a plain-language framing: away and back); the "40% of productive time" figure; that the 20-minute block was tested.
Caveat lives on the card and in the description, not in the voiceover.
