# pay-yourself-first

- Created: 2026-11-07
- Channel: Small Change Lab
- Pillar / series: Money / standard (problem -> fix, 3 steps)
- Working title: "Does Automatic Saving Beat Willpower?"
- Topic: #19 in `creative/trending-habit-topics-2026-11.xlsx` (pay yourself first, trend score 11.78). Chosen for pillar balance: episodes 1-4 were Mind, Body, Mind, Money, and the Money target (~35%) is the furthest behind. Not a repeat of `04-coin-jar-savings` (visible goal and split accounts); this one is about the default/automatic step. Alternatives: #9 lifestyle creep, #17 impulse-buy delay, #14 emergency fund.
- Central idea: saving what is left after spending usually leaves nothing. When saving happens automatically by default, many more people save. Automate a tiny transfer on payday.
- Audience: students and early-career workers who plan to save "what is left".
- Hook (outcome hook, sentence 1 <=12 words): "Saving on autopilot may help you save more." (Batch log: outcome, claim, myth, claim, outcome.)
- Payoff: three steps (pick a small amount, schedule a transfer to a separate account on payday, set it once and leave it), then "Try it today: schedule one tiny transfer for your next payday."
- Visual idea: our own take, no trend footage. Inch on payday with a paper wallet; coins drain away (problem: nothing left). Fix: a paper calendar with a payday circle, an arrow sliding a coin into a separate jar before the rest is spent. Mint tint background, coin chip, NOT FINANCIAL ADVICE tag from the cite card on.
- Loop: last frame returns to the hook layout, wallet now with the payday coin already moved to the jar.

## Source (verified 2026-11-07 via Crossref + OpenAlex abstract)
- Madrian, B. C., & Shea, D. F. (2001). The power of suggestion: Inertia in 401(k) participation and savings behavior. *The Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 116(4), 1149-1187. doi:10.1162/003355301753265543.
- Finding used (from the abstract): employees of one large US corporation; after the company switched from opt-in to automatic enrollment in its 401(k), participation was significantly higher, and many hired under automatic enrollment stuck to the default contribution rate.
- Limit line for the card: `one company` plus `one study`. **Participation percentages are NOT used**: the abstract gives no numbers and the full text was not read.
- Not claimed: any percentage or amount; that it works outside workplace plans (a bank auto-transfer is a plausible analogue, not tested); that the default rate was optimal (the paper notes many stuck to a low default); any product, bank or app.
