## Source (verified 2026-11-06 via PubMed efetch, abstract only)
- Chang, A.-M., Aeschbach, D., Duffy, J. F., & Czeisler, C. A. (2015). Evening use of light-emitting eReaders negatively affects sleep, circadian timing, and next-morning alertness. *PNAS*, 112(4), 1232-1237. doi:10.1073/pnas.1418490112. PMID 25535358.
- Finding used (from the abstract): participants reading a light-emitting eBook took longer to fall asleep, had reduced evening sleepiness, later circadian timing and reduced next-morning alertness than when reading a printed book.
- Limit line for the card: `lab study` (one study). **Sample size and the number of minutes are NOT used**: the full text was not retrievable (PMC returned no body text), so n and the minutes difference are unverified. Add `n =` only after reading the paper.
- Cite card text: tab THE STUDY / source "Chang et al., 2015 · PNAS" / finding "SLOWER TO FALL ASLEEP" / limit "one lab study".
- Not claimed: that phones, blue light or any screen type specifically caused it in real homes; that dimming lights or paper books help (steps are plausible behaviours, not tested by this study).
