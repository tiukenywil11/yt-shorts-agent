# focus-block

- Created: 2026-11-06
- Channel: Small Change Lab
- Pillar / series: Mind / standard (problem → fix, 3 steps)
- Working title: "Every Switch Costs You Time"
- Topic: the #1 topic in `creative/trending-habit-topics-2026-11.xlsx` (trend score 20.38). Recent Shorts with "study until the ice melts" visible-timer focus sessions get about 320K views/day (median of the top 5 relevant Shorts, ≤120 days old). Our angle is the science behind it: switching tasks has a time cost, so use one task with a visible timer.
- Central idea: switching tasks costs time, and the cost grows with the task's complexity. Protect one task with a short, visible focus block.
- Audience: students and early-career workers who study or work with a phone next to them.
- Hook (outcome hook, sentence 1 ≤12 words): "Every time you switch tasks, your brain pays a time cost."
- Payoff: a three-step focus block (one task + a visible 20-minute timer, the phone out of reach, a "later" note for stray thoughts), then "Try it today."
- Visual idea: our own take on the trend's visible timer. Inch's 20-minute timer is a paper ice cube that shrinks across the block. It is a generic object, and the trend's footage and compositions are not copied. The problem beat: Inch spins between a book, a phone and a laptop, and a small clock ticks up with each switch.
- Loop: the last frame returns to Inch at the desk in the hook position. The ice cube is gone (the block is finished) and the book is open (fixed state).

## Source (verified 2026-11-06 via PubMed efetch + Crossref)
- Rubinstein, J. S., Meyer, D. E., & Evans, J. E. (2001). Executive control of cognitive processes in task switching. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: Human Perception and Performance*, 27(4), 763–797. doi:10.1037/0096-1523.27.4.763. PMID 11518143.
- Finding used: across 4 lab experiments, participants were slower when they alternated between tasks than when they repeated the same task. The switching-time cost grew with rule complexity and shrank when cues showed which task came next.
- Limit line for the card: `lab tasks` (simple classification and arithmetic tasks). The participant count is not in the abstract, so no `n =` line.
- Not used: the popular "up to 40% of productive time" figure. Its original source could not be verified.
- Cite card text: source "Rubinstein et al., 2001 · J Exp Psych" / finding "SWITCHING = SLOWER" / limit "lab tasks".
