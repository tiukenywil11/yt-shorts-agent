# motivation-vs-discipline

- Created: 2026-11-06
- Channel: Small Change Lab
- Pillar / series: Mind / Habit Myths (myth -> what the research says -> real habit)
- Working title: "Do You Need Motivation to Start?"
- Topic: #2 in `creative/trending-habit-topics-2026-11.xlsx` (trend score 19.13, ~246K median views/day on the top 5 relevant Shorts). Not a repeat of `01-focus-block` (task switching) or `02-night-wind-down` (sleep).
- Central idea: the myth is that disciplined people just have more willpower. In a set of studies, better self-control went with stronger habits and less effortful resisting. A fixed cue plus a tiny start is a concrete way to build a habit.
- Audience: students and early-career workers who wait to "feel like it".
- Hook (myth/curiosity hook, sentence 1 <=12 words): "Disciplined people may not be using more willpower." (Batch-1 log: focus-block outcome, night-wind-down claim, this one myth.)
- Payoff: three steps (fixed cue, two-minute version, do it right after the cue), then "Try it today: pick one cue and do two minutes after it."
- Visual idea: our own take. Inch at a desk with a book, waiting for a "MOTIVATION" battery that sits near empty (problem). A coral MYTH stamp, then a mint ACTUALLY stamp on the cite card/habit loop. Mind tint background.
- Loop: last frame returns to the hook layout, battery replaced by a checked cue note and an open book.

## Source (verified 2026-11-06 via PubMed efetch, abstract only)
- Galla, B. M., & Duckworth, A. L. (2015). More than resisting temptation: Beneficial habits mediate the relationship between self-control and positive life outcomes. *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 109(3), 508-525. doi:10.1037/pspp0000026. PMID 25643222.
- Finding used (from the abstract): across 6 studies (total N = 2,274), people with better self-control relied more on beneficial habits, and reported less effortful inhibition. Habits linked self-control to outcomes such as homework completion and GPA.
- Limit line for the card: `n = 2,274` plus `mostly self-reported` (several measures were self-reports; the abstract also lists objective GPA).
- Cite card text: tab THE STUDY / source "Galla & Duckworth, 2015" / finding "HABITS > WILLPOWER" or "N = 2,274" / limit "linked, mostly self-reported".
- Not claimed: that habits cause success; that motivation is useless; that a cue or two-minute start was tested (the steps are plausible behaviours consistent with habit research, not tested by this study).
