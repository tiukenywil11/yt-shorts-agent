- Working title: "Does a Visible Savings Goal Help?"
- Topic: #5 in `creative/trending-habit-topics-2026-11.xlsx` (coin jar / piggy-bank savings challenges, trend score 16.81, ~41K median views/day on the top 5 relevant Shorts). Chosen for pillar balance: episodes 1-3 were Mind, Body, Mind; Money (target ~35%) has none yet. Not a repeat of any earlier episode. Alternatives if the creator prefers a Mind/Body habit: #4 phone screen time, #6 procrastination.
- Central idea: money kept in one unnamed pile is easy to spend. In one study, a savings goal with a visual reminder, and money split into two parts, went with higher saving. A named goal you can see is a concrete way to try this.
- Audience: students and early-career workers who mean to save but never see the money.
- Hook (claim hook, sentence 1 <=12 words): "A visible savings goal may help you save more." (Batch log: outcome, claim, myth, claim. Fine, different pillar.)
- Payoff: three steps (name one goal, keep a visible reminder, split the saving into two parts), then "Try it today: name one savings goal and put it where you can see it."
- Visual idea: our own take, no trend footage copied. Inch beside a plain paper jar with no label (problem: coins vanish). Fix: a label tag with the goal, a paper photo of the goal taped on, a second small jar. Mint tint background, coin chip, NOT FINANCIAL ADVICE tag from the cite card on.
- Loop: last frame returns to the hook layout, jar now labelled and the second jar beside it.

## Source (verified 2026-11-07 via Crossref + OpenAlex abstract)
- Soman, D., & Cheema, A. (2011). Earmarking and partitioning: Increasing saving by low-income households. *Journal of Marketing Research*, 48(SPL), S14-S22. doi:10.1509/jmkr.48.spl.s14.
- Finding used (from the abstract): the study examined earmarked money among low-income consumers. A visual reminder of the savings goal increased the savings rate, and people saved more when earmarked money was split into two accounts than when it was pooled in one.
- Limit line for the card: `low-income consumers` plus `one study`. Sample size not seen in the abstract, so no n is stated.
- Not claimed: that jars or coins specifically were tested; that it works for everyone; any amount saved; that three steps were tested together.
