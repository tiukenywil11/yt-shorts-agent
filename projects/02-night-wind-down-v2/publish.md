## 2. Description

Same bedtime, same dim room: in one small lab study, reading on a light-emitting screen meant nearly ten minutes longer to fall asleep than reading on paper.
Source: Chang, A.-M., Aeschbach, D., Duffy, J. F., & Czeisler, C. A. (2015). Evening use of light-emitting eReaders negatively affects sleep, circadian timing, and next-morning alertness. PNAS, 112(4), 1232-1237. doi:10.1073/pnas.1418490112
NOT MEDICAL ADVICE. One lab study, n = 12 adults (about 25.65 vs 15.75 minutes to fall asleep). Only a printed book vs a light-emitting eReader was tested, so results may differ for you. Ask a clinician if you have a sleep condition.
