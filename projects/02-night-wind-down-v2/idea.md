## Source (full text read 2026-11-09, PMC4313820)
Chang, Aeschbach, Duffy & Czeisler (2015), PNAS 112(4), 1232-1237. doi:10.1073/pnas.1418490112. 12 healthy young adults, randomized crossover, 14-day in-patient study. Reading a light-emitting eReader vs a printed book before bed: sleep latency 25.65 +/- 18.78 min vs 15.75 +/- 13.09 min (P = 0.009), "nearly 10 min longer". Also less REM sleep (109.04 vs 120.86 min, P = 0.03), lower melatonin, later circadian timing, lower next-morning alertness. The paper notes no difference in total sleep duration.
Card: "Chang et al., 2015 - PNAS" / "SCREEN: ~26 MIN TO SLEEP, PAPER: ~16" / "n = 12 - one lab study". Body tag: NOT MEDICAL ADVICE.
Not claimed: that phones or other screens behave the same as the iPad used; that dimming lights or the same-order routine was tested (only paper vs eReader was); any sleep-disorder, melatonin or supplement advice. Say "nearly ten minutes", never "ten".
