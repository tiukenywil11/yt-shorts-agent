# focus-block-v2 (side project: rebuild of 01-focus-block; 01 is untouched)

- Created: 2026-11-08
- Channel: Small Change Lab | Mind | standard (problem -> fix, 3 steps)
- Working title: "This Is What One Phone Check Does to Your Focus"
- Central idea: switching tasks has a time cost (Rubinstein et al., 2001, same verified source as 01). Show the cost on a visible paper ice-cube timer: every switch makes the ice grow back.
- Audience: students and early-career workers with a phone next to them.
- Hook (visual, frame 0): the ice-cube timer is already melting at the desk; Inch reaches for the phone. Narration: "Watch what one phone check does to this timer."
- Payoff: 3-step focus block (one task + visible 20-min timer, phone face down, "later" list), then the ice melts fully.

## Changes vs 01 (from the critique)
1. Hook rebuilt around the timer, stakes from frame 0.
2. Ice cube is the star from frame 0; each switch makes it regrow (a visual metaphor for the cost, not a measured claim).
3. Length ~26 s (was 39.6 s). The "harder task" line is cut. Citation is a ~1.5 s lower-third, not a held card.
4. Tension beat: "just a quick peek" shatters the timer, then the fix is relief.
5. Caveat moved out of the voiceover: on-screen tag "ONE LAB STUDY - SIMPLE TASKS" on the citation, plus the description. Wording stays "in one study / in the lab"; never "proven".
6. Ending: ice fully melts, the video ends ~0.1 s after the voiceover (no trailing loop scene).
7. Voice: same Kokoro af_heart (no better engine installed); sound design uses existing SFX only.

## Source (verified in 01, 2026-11-06 via PubMed + Crossref)
Rubinstein, Meyer & Evans (2001), JEP: Human Perception and Performance, 27(4), 763-797. doi:10.1037/0096-1523.27.4.763. Finding: in the lab, people were slower alternating between tasks than repeating one. Card: "Rubinstein et al., 2001 · J Exp Psych" / "SWITCHING = SLOWER" / "lab tasks".
